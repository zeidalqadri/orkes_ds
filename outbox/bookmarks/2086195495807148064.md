## Why

- Model behavior, available tools, provider limits, and agent-loop contracts change faster than long-lived instructions. A rule that once prevented a failure may later constrain a capable model or preserve an obsolete workaround.
- Existing `shared_learnings.md`, `PROMPT.md`, skills, and expert records are valuable operational memory. Deleting them wholesale discards hard-won safety rules, portal-specific knowledge, and recovery evidence.
- The durable pattern is to compare the current instruction stack against a lean baseline on real replayable tasks, then keep only rules that still improve correctness, safety, speed, or operator usefulness.

## Adopt / adapt

1. **Run a quarterly instruction recertification, plus an event-triggered review after model/provider/tool migrations.** Inventory `PROMPT.md`, `AGENTS.md`/`CLAUDE.md`, skills, expert cards, and recent shared learnings. Classify each item as retain, rewrite, archive, or remove; never bulk-delete by age alone.
2. **Use replay fixtures to prove instruction value.** For each candidate rule, compare the current stack with a minimal baseline on representative work: bookmark intake, PM2 incident handling, SmartGEP auth diagnosis, API-contract repair, and safe write workflows. Retain rules only where results improve a stated acceptance check without increasing cost or latency.
3. **Separate invariant controls from model-era heuristics.** Preserve security boundaries, secret handling, approval gates, exact file/DB contracts, and portal facts as durable controls. Tag provider/model-specific prompts, formatting nudges, and workaround instructions with source, observed failure, owner, and `review_after` date.
4. **Keep prompt assembly lean and traceable.** Promote only distilled, repeated evidence into always-loaded context; leave dated incident details in searchable records. This complements the existing memory-layering and byte-stable prompt-prefix work instead of adding another memory source.
5. **Apply the same gate to skills and expert workflows.** A skill remains installed only if its commands, dependencies, and output contract pass an executable smoke test on the current backend. Archive failed or superseded variants with the replacement rationale so the fleet does not rediscover the same dead end.

## Do not adopt

- Do not schedule unconditional deletion of `CLAUDE.md`, `PROMPT.md`, skills, or shared learnings.
- Do not let an LLM prune safety controls without fixture evidence and an explicit human approval boundary for operational changes.
- Do not treat a newer model as automatically better at local portals, credentials, or deterministic remediation; keep observed contracts and test them after upgrades.
