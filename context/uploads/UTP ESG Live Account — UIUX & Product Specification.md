## UTP ESG Live Account — UI/UX & Product Specification  
**Version:** 1.1 **Last Updated:** 03 May 2026 **Owner:** UTP Sustainability Office **Status:** Enhanced specification for MVP development **Product Type:** One-page interactive public ESG reporting and feedback outlet  
   
⸻  
   
## 0. Executive Summary  
The **UTP ESG Live Account** is a one-page public interface that presents UTP’s ESG performance as an accountable, evidence-linked, challengeable record.  
It is not a promotional ESG dashboard. It is not a sustainability microsite. It is not a Power BI maze. It is a **public ESG account book**.  
Its core job is to answer five questions:  
1. **What is UTP’s ESG position this month?**  
2. **What is UTP’s ESG position this month?**  
3. **What changed compared with the previous period, baseline, and target?**  
4. **What changed compared with the previous period, baseline, and target?**  
5. **Where did each number come from?**  
6. **Where did each number come from?**  
7. **Who owns the number?**  
8. **Who owns the number?**  
9. **How can stakeholders challenge, correct, or improve the account?**  
10. **How can stakeholders challenge, correct, or improve the account?**  
The Live Account should sit on top of a deeper ESG data and evidence system, because the project resources repeatedly show that modern university ESG systems are moving toward automated data pipelines, real-time campus sensing, AI-assisted validation, audit-ready evidence repositories, and living-lab functionality.    
The visible public product, however, should remain disciplined:  
**One page. One account. One feedback loop.**  
   
⸻  
   
## 1. Product Definition  
## 1.1 Product Name  
**UTP ESG Live Account**  
## 1.2 Product Format  
A **one-page interactive public reporting and feedback outlet**.  
The interface is vertically structured. It may use sticky anchors, drawers, filters and modals, but it should not become a multi-page public dashboard.  
## 1.3 Primary Purpose  
**Public ESG accountability.**  
## 1.4 Secondary Purposes  
* Stakeholder feedback and challenge handling.  
* Evidence transparency.  
* Monthly ESG close.  
* Public trust-building.  
* Restatement transparency.  
* Safe ESG data download.  
* Methodology disclosure.  
* Demonstration of UTP’s “walk the talk” sustainability discipline.  
## 1.5 Core Philosophy  
**This is not a brochure. This is UTP’s ESG account.**  
The page should behave like a sustainability ledger: every number has a source, method, owner, date, target, variance and evidence state.  
## 1.6 Strategic Rationale  
UTP is a strong candidate for this model because the project resources identify UTP’s net-zero campus ambition, solar PV, Green Electricity Tariff participation, energy-efficiency work, waste-diversion activity, water savings, PETRONAS alignment, and living-lab potential as foundations for a value-multiplying ESG system.    
The Live Account is the public-facing expression of that discipline. The underlying repository may support internal dashboards, AI, assurance, reporting, research and curriculum, but the public outlet must remain focused on transparent accounting.  
   
⸻  
   
## 2. Design Principles  
## 2.1 One Page, Five Movements  
The full public experience consists of five stacked movements:  
1. **Account Status**  
2. **Account Status**  
3. **The ESG Ledger**  
4. **The ESG Ledger**  
5. **Proof of Claims**  
6. **Proof of Claims**  
7. **Feedback & Challenges**  
8. **Feedback & Challenges**  
9. **Monthly Close**  
10. **Monthly Close**  
This matches the earlier Live Account direction: the public-facing product should collapse ESG reporting into a single accountable page rather than presenting a sprawling dashboard experience.    
## 2.2 No Dashboard Bloat  
Avoid:  
* multiple public dashboards;  
* “Explore E / S / G” landing pages;  
* executive-style public cockpits;  
* public AI co-pilots;  
* public scenario simulators;  
* vague sustainability storytelling;  
* decorative ESG imagery;  
* excessive navigation;  
* disconnected PDF reports.  
The public page should show the account. The internal platform can hold the machinery.  
## 2.3 Accounting Discipline  
No ESG number may appear publicly unless it has:  
* source;  
* method;  
* owner;  
* reporting period;  
* baseline or comparator;  
* target, where applicable;  
* variance;  
* evidence status;  
* approval status;  
* last updated timestamp.  
## 2.4 Public-First, Evidence-Backed  
The interface is designed for:  
* students;  
* staff;  
* faculty;  
* alumni;  
* community members;  
* partners;  
* PETRONAS-linked stakeholders;  
* ranking / accreditation observers;  
* public users.  
However, public access does not mean full access to raw data. Sensitive data must remain private, with public summaries where appropriate.  
## 2.5 Collect Once, Report Many Times  
Every metric should be structured so that it can support multiple reporting outputs: public dashboarding, monthly close, GRI, SDGs, QS Sustainability, THE Impact, STARS-aligned evidence, PETRONAS-aligned reporting, board reporting and assurance packs. The project resources identify this reuse principle as central to avoiding duplicative ESG reporting.

## 4.3 Status Statement  
**Example**  
**UTP ESG Account Status: At Risk but Improving**  
UTP is progressing on renewable electricity, food-waste diversion and water savings, but remains behind the required annual emissions-reduction pathway for Net Zero Carbon Campus 2030.  
**Tone Rules**  
The statement must be:  
* direct;  
* evidence-led;  
* plain-language;  
* honest about gaps;  
* not promotional.  
Avoid:  
“UTP proudly continues its amazing sustainability journey.”  
Use:  
“UTP is off track on energy intensity this month. Corrective actions are listed in the Monthly Close.”

## 5. Section 2 — The ESG Ledger  
## 5.1 Purpose  
The ESG Ledger is the heart of the Live Account.  
It should behave like an account book: every ESG item is a line item with target, actual, variance, owner, evidence and challenge path.  
The project resources emphasise that credible ESG repositories should hold source data, calculated metrics, methodology notes, emissions factors, evidence documents, approvals, version history, audit trails and framework mappings.   The public ledger is the simplified front end of that discipline.  
   
⸻  
   
## 5.2 Default Layout  
A single table-like ledger with collapsible rows.  
**Default Display**  
* Shows headline metrics.  
* Uses tabs only as filters, not navigation.  
* Allows row expansion.  
* Allows challenge per metric.  
* Shows evidence and verification state.  
**Tabs / Filters**  
* All  
* Environment  
* Social  
* Governance  
* Off Track  
* Verified  
* Open Challenges  
* Restated  
Tabs refine the same ledger in place.  
   
⸻  
   
## 5.3 Ledger Columns  
Every public ledger row must include:  

| Column    | Description               | Example             |
| --------- | ------------------------- | ------------------- |
| Metric    | Public metric name        | Solar PV Generation |
| Current   | Latest value              | 2.1 GWh             |
| Previous  | Previous reporting period | 1.9 GWh             |
| Baseline  | Starting point            | 1.5 GWh, 2023       |
| Target    | Current or future target  | 2.0 GWh             |
| Variance  | Difference from target    | +5%                 |
| Trend     | Direction of movement     | ↑ / ↓ / →           |
| Owner     | Responsible unit          | Facilities          |
| Evidence  | Evidence availability     | Evidence-linked     |
| Status    | Assurance state           | Owner-approved      |
| Challenge | Feedback action           | Challenge           |
  
**5.4 Required Accounting Rule**  
No ledger row may be published without:  
* metric ID;  
* owner;  
* current value;  
* reporting period;  
* source;  
* methodology;  
* evidence state;  
* last updated timestamp.  
If the value is missing, the row may still appear, but only with a transparent missing-data state.

## 8. Metric Governance Rules  
These rules should be treated as non-negotiable.  
1. No public metric may be published without an assigned owner.  
2. No public metric may show as “on track” without a defined target or pathway.  
3. No public metric may show as “verified” without independent verification evidence.  
4. No positive ESG claim may appear in the hero section unless the metric is at least owner-approved and evidence-linked.  
5. Every restatement must show original value, revised value, reason, date, owner and evidence note.  
6. Metrics with unresolved material evidence gaps must be labelled provisional or withdrawn.  
7. Every metric must have a public methodology summary.  
8. Sensitive evidence must be replaced with a public evidence summary.  
9. Every challenge must receive a tracking ID.  
10. Every monthly close must disclose unresolved issues, restatements and methodology changes.  
11. No metric may be deleted from the public account without a withdrawal note unless legal or security restrictions apply.  
12. Every published value must be reproducible from retained source data and calculation logic.

## 10.7 Privacy and Moderation  
The interface must state:

* attachments may be reviewed but not made public by default.

## 11. Section 5 — Monthly Close  
## 11.1 Purpose  
Make ESG accountable to a recurring operating rhythm, similar to finance close.  
The project resources identify audit-ready repositories, traceable reporting lineage and repeatable data governance as key features of next-generation university ESG systems.   The monthly close is the public ritual that demonstrates this discipline.  
   
⸻  
   
## 11.2 Monthly Close Summary  
**Example**  
**May 2026 ESG Close**

* 9 stakeholder issues closed

## 31. Final Enhanced Product Proposition  
The final proposition should be:  
**UTP ESG Live Account is a one-page interactive public ESG account that publishes UTP’s sustainability position, ledger metrics, evidence states, variances, restatements, unresolved gaps and stakeholder challenges in a monthly accountable format.**  
It is designed to make UTP’s ESG claims inspectable.  
The product succeeds when any stakeholder can point to a number and ask:  
**Where did this come from?**  
And the page can answer:  
**Here is the source, method, owner, date, target, variance, evidence state, challenge history and latest close action.**  
That is the “walk the talk” standard.
